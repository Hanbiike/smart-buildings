Every morning, buildings wake up
hungry.
Forty percent of America's carbon
comes from buildings like this
one.
Heating and cooling eats forty
to sixty percent of that —
fifteen percent of all energy
on Earth.
And most buildings still run
like it's 1980, because a
controller tuned for one building
fails everywhere else.
Meet the facility manager.
One building, one headache — until
one policy learns to run
them all.
This is our building:
three floors, thirty-nine zones, four
air handlers.
Every room has a damper
and a reheat valve.
The policy sees them all —
and nothing else is hardcoded.
Six AM.
The building wakes.
Air handlers spin up, dampers
sweep open, and every zone
glides into its comfort band
before the first coffee is
poured.
Noon.
A heat wave hammers the
glass.
The policy pours cooling exactly
where bodies are, trimming each
damper five-minute step by five-minute
step.
Then the cold snap hits.
Reheat answers in seconds.
Zero drift.
Zero complaints.
Evening.
People leave;
the policy widens the eco
band and lets the building
sleep — warm where it matters,
dark where it doesn't.
One day, one policy, no
tuning.
How?
One neural policy reads the
floor plan and live sensors,
then commands every handler, valve,
and boiler — same brain, any
building.
