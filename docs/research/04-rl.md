### 1.2 The numbers people quote less: RL often loses to RBC and MPC

The honest literature — standardized benchmarks with fixed baselines — is much less
flattering to model-free RL:

- **Sinergym systematic evaluation** (UGR, EnergyPlus 23.1, 2024): in `5ZoneAutoDXVAV`
  (the closest public analogue to our VAV problem) **the rule-based controller beat
  SAC, PPO, and TD3 on the comfort–energy balance**. In `2ZoneDataCenterHVAC`, TD3
  had the best reward but with higher energy savings *and* greater comfort violations
  — i.e. it traded on the comfort cliff. SAC had the best training curves overall but
  still lost to RBC on the 5-zone VAV case. PPO was the most stable learner but
  converged slowest.
  Sources: [arXiv 2401.05737](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2401.05737),
  [compiled PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.05737),
  [Linnk summary](https://linnk.ai/es/insight/building-energy-optimization/comprehensive-evaluation-of-deep-reinforcement-learning-algorithms-for-efficient-hvac-control-in-buildings-SxwFsen-/),
  [Sinergym testbed paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08293v1).
- **BOPTEST hydronic heat pump** (Wang et al. 2023, summarized in the Polito MPC
  validation review): of DDPG, DDQN, SAC, **only DDPG beat the baseline** in both
  typical and peak scenarios; **MPC beat every RL controller in both scenarios**
  "because it can take the best [actions] and manage system state."
  Sources: [Energies 17(20):5117 review table](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/17/20/5117/html),
  [R Discovery summary](https://discovery.researcher.life/article/comparison-of-reinforcement-learning-and-model-predictive-control-for-building-energy-system-optimization/0f9976ea759e3f8b99cee27d766f4204).
- **End-to-end DRL, 5-zone office** (Zhang group, MDPI Urban Science 6(3):52):
  12.8% HVAC savings on the *training* building, but only with a hand-enforced
  night-setback rule (the agent never learned setback itself — overfitting), and it
  **failed on the held-out test building** on both comfort and energy — again
  attributed to overfitting.
  Source: [MDPI Urb. Sci. 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2411-9660/6/3/52).
- **PPO vs SAC on identical BOPTEST MDP** (arXiv 2608.09453, 2026): SAC discovered a
  continuous-modulation (inverter-style) policy with zero compressor start-ups/day;
  **PPO collapsed to bang-bang control that cycled more than the baseline**. On
  out-of-sample BOPTEST KPIs the SAC policy cut thermal discomfort up to **90.7%**
  for a modest bounded cost increase.
  Sources: [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09453v1),
  [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09453v1.pdf).
- **SACEM vs PPO/SAC/TD3 under extreme heat** (Sustainability 2026): vanilla
  PPO/SAC/TD3 "struggle to maintain comfort under extreme outdoor temperatures";
  the hybrid SACEM reached 95.8% comfort score with −21% energy / −21.3% cost vs the
  strongest baseline. Lesson: pure model-free degrades exactly at the tails.
  Source: [MDPI Sustainability 18(2):1036](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/18/2/1036).

**Pattern.** Model-free RL reliably beats *weak* baselines (fixed schedules, on/off,
poorly tuned RBC) by 10–30%, roughly ties well-tuned RBC, and loses to MPC on
standardized testbeds. The 20–70% and 30–40% figures in reviews come from weak
baselines and narrow sim setups. Our competition analogue: the "leader at 0.00521 vs
our 0.01323" gap is far more likely MPC/planning-shaped than policy-gradient-shaped.

## 2. The sample-efficiency problem (and why it mostly does not bind us)

### 2.1 What the literature says

- The standard diagnosis (Model-Free HVAC Control review, Energies 16(20):7124):
  Q-learning, DQN, and vanilla policy gradients are sample-inefficient; each
  environment interaction in a real building costs wall-clock time and occupant
  comfort, so "costly and time-consuming" training blocks deployment. PPO/TRPO are
  more stable but still on-policy and data-hungry.
  Source: [MDPI Energies review](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/16/20/7124/htm).
- Neural-network model-based approach (Zhang et al., ACM BuildSys 2019): the
  model-based method improves sample efficiency **10× over PPO** on the same HVAC
  scheduling task.
  Sources: [arXiv 1910.05313](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05313v1),
  [ar5iv HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1910.05313).
- CLUE (BuildSys 2023 best-paper line, arXiv 2407.12195): GP-based MBRL converges
  **>30× faster than previous SOTA** and produces a more accurate model from the
  same data.
  Sources: [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12195v2),
  [CLUE slides](https://ryeii.github.io/assets/clue_slides.pdf),
  [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3600100.3623742).
- Sinergym testbed paper: "PPO proved to be the most stable… followed by TD3 and
  SAC," but PPO needs the most samples; off-policy SAC/TD3 learn faster per step
  and reach higher training reward. General continuous-control wisdom (SB3
  benchmarks): SAC reaches in ~1/10th the environment steps what PPO needs millions
  for.
  Sources: [Sinergym testbed](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08293v1),
  [SB3 SAC-vs-PPO discussion](https://temporalbook.icsgen-ai.org/part-6-sequential-decision-making/module-25-deep-reinforcement-learning/section-25.3.html).

### 2.2 Why it does not bind *us*

Every sample-efficiency argument above assumes **real-building interaction cost**.
We have a fast simulator with unlimited resets. In competition terms, sample
efficiency converts only into wall-clock training budget, and even PPO-scale budgets
(millions of steps) are affordable if the simulator steps in milliseconds. The
binding constraints for us are instead: (a) the stiff comfort cliff in the reward,
(b) generalization across hidden buildings/weather (the Sinergym 5-zone RBC result
and the Urban-Science overfitting failure are direct warnings), and (c) optimizer
stability on that landscape — which is exactly where our PPO diverged. So: do not
pick algorithms for sample efficiency; pick them for **stability + generalization +
constraint handling**.

## 3. Safety and constraints during learning

Real deployments cannot violate comfort/equipment constraints while exploring. The
literature converged on four mechanisms, all relevant to us as *reward-shaping /
action-projection* ideas even though we face no real safety risk:

1. **Action-range + rate limits (simplest, works).** Zhang et al. (BuildSys 2019)
   "limit the actions within safe range and the maximum absolute change of actions
   according to prior knowledge." This is the direct ancestor of action-delta
   penalties and slew-rate limits.
   Source: [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3360322.3360861).
2. **MPC safety filter / shield.** An MPC (or data-driven MPC) projects or replaces
   the DRL action whenever it would violate constraints; DRL explores optimality,
   MPC guarantees feasibility. Variants: "safe DRL" (DRL+MPC,
   [ScienceDirect S0306261924017112](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0306261924017112));
   runtime shielding framework for temperature constraints
   ([Sci. Rep. 2025, PMC11882997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11882997/));
   data-driven MPC safety filter for heating/DHW
   ([alphaxiv 2604.16033](https://pdfs.assets.alphaxiv.org/2604.16033v1.pdf)).
3. **Uncertainty-aware planning (CLUE).** GP dynamics model with epistemic
   uncertainty → risk-aware MPPI/MPC that avoids state-actions the model is unsure
   about. Beats deep-ensemble MBRL on comfort violations *despite higher model
   error* — robustness comes from knowing what you don't know.
   Source: [arXiv 2407.12195v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12195v2).
4. **Barrier certificates / constrained policy optimization.** Neural barrier
   certificates on BOPTEST match PPO/RBC energy with formal safety flavor
   ([Energies 18(19):5313](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/18/19/5313)).
   Dual safety policies (penalty-augmented reward) cut >15% vs PID
   ([Buildings 13:580](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/buildings/buildings-13-00580/article_deploy/buildings-13-00580.pdf)).

**Competition translation:** "safety" = never falling off the comfort cliff. The
shielding literature says: don't ask the policy to *learn* the cliff — **project**
actions (rate-limit, clamp, MPC-filter) so the cliff is unreachable, then optimize
the smooth remainder. Our damper ≥10% / discharge-cap / recirc-cap handling should
be hard projections, not learned behavior.

## 4. Why model-free policy gradients struggle on stiff comfort landscapes

This section explains our reproduced PPO divergence. Four compounding mechanisms,
each documented:

### 4.1 The reward is a cliff, and PG needs smooth signal

Our reward: comfort slope ≈ 0.18/K vs near-flat energy slope. That is a
**stiff two-scale landscape**: a sharp comfort penalty next to a weak energy
gradient. Policy-gradient estimators have variance ∝ reward scale; the cliff
dominates every batch's advantage estimates, so gradients either explode (step off
the cliff → huge negative advantage → destructive update) or vanish (overly
conservative steps that never explore the energy-saving direction). The Sinergym
evaluation shows the empirical footprint: TD3 "higher savings but greater comfort
violations," SAC temperature-threshold riding in 5ZoneAutoDXVAV — agents learn to
*sit on the cliff edge*, the highest-variance place in the landscape.
Sources: [Sinergym PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.05737),
[UGR thesis PDF](https://digibug.ugr.es/bitstream/handle/10481/92629/s10462-024-10819-x.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed).

### 4.2 PPO's failure mode on HVAC is bang-bang collapse

The cleanest recent demonstration (arXiv 2608.09453): identical MDP, SAC learns
smooth modulation, **PPO collapses to bang-bang and cycles more than baseline**.
Mechanism: PPO's clipped surrogate + on-policy sampling cannot represent/retain the
smooth intermediate actions once an early bang-bang behavior gets a lucky batch —
clipping then *prevents escape* from the collapsed policy. On/off cycling is
precisely the behavior that destroys comfort scores on stiff landscapes (overshoot
oscillations across the cliff). Our divergence is very likely this same collapse in
continuous-action form (saturation at action bounds).
Source: [arXiv 2608.09453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09453v1).

### 4.3 Entropy is the difference between SAC and PPO here

SAC's maximum-entropy objective with automatic temperature tuning keeps the policy
stochastic and "prevents premature collapse onto a narrow deterministic action,"
making it "remarkably insensitive to hyperparameters"; twin critics (shared with
TD3) control overestimation on the cliff edge.
Source: [Temporal-AI textbook §25.4](https://temporalbook.icsgen-ai.org/part-6-sequential-decision-making/module-25-deep-reinforcement-learning/section-25.4.html).
Practical corollary, confirmed across benchmarks: on HVAC-like smooth-control tasks,
**SAC ≥ TD3 > PPO** in final reward per environment step, with PPO only winning on
"stability of the learning curve" (a consolation prize when it converges to bang-bang).

### 4.4 Missing normalization is fatal on two-scale rewards

SB3's canonical recipe — `VecNormalize(norm_obs=True, norm_reward=True)`, advantage
normalization `(A−μ)/σ`, reward scaling by rolling discounted-sum σ — exists
exactly for two-scale landscapes like ours. Without it, the value head fits the
0.18/K comfort scale and the energy gradient is numerical noise; with per-batch
advantage whitening, both scales contribute. Andrychowicz et al. (2020) show reward
and state normalization are "essential to achieve learning stability and
convergence, especially when rewards or states fluctuate by orders of magnitude" —
our comfort/energy ratio is exactly that.
Sources: [SB3 examples](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/guide/examples.html),
[Andrychowicz-citing study](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/abs/2301.12802),
[practitioner lessons](https://deepwiki.com/adrische/Reimplementing-PPO/7.3-lessons-for-practitioners).

**Verdict on our PPO divergence:** expected, not bad luck. Unnormalized two-scale
reward + on-policy clipping + no entropy pressure = bang-bang/saturation collapse.
Any model-free retry must be SAC-based with full normalization (see §8).

## 5. Model-based RL for buildings

### 5.1 The generic MBRL toolbox (PETS / MBPO / MuZero / Dreamer)

- **PETS** (Chua et al. 2018): probabilistic ensemble dynamics + trajectory sampling
  (CEM) + MPC-style replanning. Reaches model-free asymptotic performance on
  MuJoCo locomotion with **orders of magnitude fewer samples** (e.g. half-cheetah
  at a fraction of SAC/PPO samples). The ensemble captures aleatoric uncertainty;
  CEM planning needs no policy network at all.
  Sources: [MBRL-Lib (PETS+MBPO reference impl.)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/abs/2104.10159),
  [industry-near MBRL study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.11971),
  [PETS/MBPO explainer](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2309.11089).
- **MBPO** (Janner et al. 2019): PETS-style model generating short branched
  rollouts to feed a model-free learner (SAC). SOTA sample efficiency at
  publication; the "best of both" template: model for data, SAC for the policy.
  Sources: same as above.
- **MuZero / EfficientZero**: latent dynamics + MCTS planning; Sampled EfficientZero
  is now the go-to *independent MBRL baseline* in HVAC papers (used at equal budget
  in the LDLP study, §5.2).
  Source: [Applsci. 16(18):9131](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/18/9131).
- **DreamerV3** (Hafner et al.): latent RSSM world model + actor-critic on imagined
  trajectories; ~20× sample reduction vs model-free on control suites, 2–3× from
  discrete latents alone. No direct HVAC application found in search — a genuine
  gap, but note Dreamer's strength (pixels, long-horizon sparse reward) mismatches
  HVAC (low-dim states, dense stiff reward, short effective horizon from thermal
  mass ≈ hours).
  Sources: [DreamerV3 200-line impl. notes](https://tildalice.io/dreamerv3-world-model-rl-pytorch-implementation/),
  [ExpertBeacon summary](https://expertbeacon.com/google-deepminds-dreamer-v3-a-leap-forward-in-reinforcement-learning/),
  [VITALab DreamerV3 notes](https://vitalab.github.io/article/2023/01/19/DreamerV3).

### 5.2 MBRL built specifically for HVAC (the results that matter)

- **MB²C** (Ding et al., arXiv 2302.00725): ensemble NN dynamics + **MPPI**
  (Model Predictive Path Integral — importance-weighted action-sequence sampling
  that scales to multi-zone action dims where CEM struggles). EnergyPlus 5-zone
  office: **+8.23% energy savings over the previous SOTA MBRL** at equal comfort.
  This is the multi-zone MBRL reference design.
  Sources: [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2302.00725),
  [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/368159542_Multi-zone_HVAC_Control_with_Model-Based_Deep_Reinforcement_Learning).
- **CLUE** (BuildSys 2023 / arXiv 2407.12195): GP dynamics + meta-kernel learning
  (domain knowledge sets GP hyperparams across buildings) + uncertainty-aware safe
  control. >30× faster convergence than prior SOTA; lower comfort-violation rates
  than deep-ensemble MBRL *even with higher model error*. Key lesson for us:
  **uncertainty-aware planning beats a more accurate model planned over naively**.
  Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12195v2),
  [Linnk summary](https://linnk.ai/nl/insight/machine-learning/clue-a-model-based-reinforcement-learning-system-for-data-efficient-and-safe-hvac-control-using-gaussian-processes-mvRjMhyo/),
  [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3600100.3623742).
- **LDLP** (Applsci. 2026): latent dynamics + MCTS planning on Sinergym 5Zone across
  hot/mixed/cool climates, vs PPO/SAC/TD3/Sampled-EfficientZero at equal budget.
  Comfort-normalized energy: **−4% to −26% vs PPO/SAC under standard reward,
  −1% to −19% under occupancy-gated reward**. Also the paper that documents
  occupancy-gating the comfort penalty (penalty applies only when occupied) —
  independent confirmation of our occ_gated direction.
  Source: [Applsci. 16(18):9131](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/18/9131).
- **NN-dynamics + MPC shooting** (Zhang et al., BuildSys 2019): learned NN dynamics
  + random-shooting MPC, safe action/range limits; 10× sample efficiency over PPO
  (§2.1). The minimal viable MBRL-for-HVAC recipe.
  Source: [arXiv 1910.05313](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05313v1).

### 5.3 MBRL vs MFRL on HVAC, summarized

"Model-free RL faces the challenge of sample efficiency which causes long-time
training and restricts applications" (comparative-study abstract), while MBRL "often
lags behind MFRL in control performance" in raw asymptotic reward on the *same*
building but wins on data, safety, and transfer (MB²C paper's own framing).
Sources: [R Discovery](https://discovery.researcher.life/article/comparative-study-of-modelbased-and-modelfree-reinforcement-learning-control-performance-in-hvac-systems/41372bb3e3213889aa873673cf0511c0),
[MB²C PDF](https://s.bingheai.cn/pdf/2302.00725).
For us — free simulator, hidden buildings — the tradeoff resolves clearly: the
policy-generalization failures (§1.2) argue for **planning at decision time**
(MPC/CEM/MPPI over a learned or physics model), which re-optimizes per state and
cannot "overfit a policy," rather than amortizing into a network.

## 6. Offline RL on logged building data

- **CQL is the backbone that works.** "Experimental evaluation of offline RL for
  HVAC" (arXiv 2408.07986): among offline algorithms, **CQL-based HVAC controllers
  achieve the best reward performance**; selected as backbone for all follow-up
  experiments.
  Sources: [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.07986),
  [AI-models summary](https://www.aimodels.fyi/papers/arxiv/experimental-evaluation-offline-reinforcement-learning-hvac-control).
- **But offline RL is data-quality-capped.** Sci. Rep. 2025 review: learned agents'
  "performance strongly depends on the quality of the historical data… tend to
  perform poorly due to distributional shift between the historical data and the
  learned policy, and may have limited improvement even with fine-tuning via
  online [interaction]."
  Source: [PMC11882997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11882997/).
- **The pragmatic hybrid: imitate, then refine.** Gnu-RL (differentiable MPC policy
  → imitate historical controller → PPO-refine end-to-end on task objective) beat
  both pure imitation and pure RL, and beat system-ID + adaptive MPC ("more
  effective to optimize task objectives end-to-end"). Heterogeneous-expert-guided
  training (Sci. Rep. 2025) similarly bootstraps RL from diverse expert
  demonstrations + runtime shield.
  Sources: [Frontiers Gnu-RL](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/built-environment/articles/10.3389/fbuil.2020.562239),
  [Sci. Rep. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11882997/).
- Data-center analogue (ICLR 2025): offline RL (IQL/CQL/FISOR) vs PID/MPC/CCA on a
  real testbed with broad historical coverage — works when the dataset covers the
  state-action space, which building logs rarely do (RBC logs are narrow).
  Source: [OpenReview PDF](https://openreview.net:443/pdf?id=W8xukd70cU).

**Competition translation:** offline RL is a weak fit for us. We have no logged
leader trajectories to imitate, our own RBC logs are narrow (distributional shift
binds hard), and we have a free simulator — the exact condition where offline RL's
raison d'être vanishes. The one portable idea: **behavior-clone our best occ_gated
controller as a policy initialization / residual center**, then refine online
(Gnu-RL-style), rather than learning from scratch.

## 7. Reward shaping and normalization practice

The literature's reward recipe is strikingly uniform — and it validates several of
our design choices while flagging one we missed:

1. **Weighted sum, energy vs comfort, with comfort dominant.** `r = −α·E − β·comfort_violation`
   (or convex combination of OPPD and E_HVAC with tunable τ/β/ρ/λ). Comfort weight
   ≫ energy weight in every successful study; BOPTEST-hydronic DQN work uses
   ω=100 on discomfort vs cost. Our (0.6/0.2/0.2) is in-family.
   Sources: [arXiv 1901.04693](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1901.04693),
   [MDPI Urban Sci. 6(3):52](https://www.mdpi.com/2411-9660/6/3/52),
   [MDPI Buildings 13:580](https://www.Mdpi.Com/2075-5309/13/3/580),
   [asymmetric-DDT paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01641v1).
2. **Occupancy-gated comfort penalty.** The LDLP paper explicitly modifies "a reward
   formulation common in prior HVAC RL work whose comfort penalty remains active
   even when the building is unoccupied, gating that penalty by occupancy." This is
   our occ_gated idea, published independently — strong external validation.
   Source: [Applsci. 16(18):9131 PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/applsci/applsci-16-09131/article_deploy/applsci-16-09131.pdf?version=1789457351).
3. **Scale/normalize before weighting.** Sinergym's reward normalizes power and
   temperature-violation terms so neither dominates numerically
   ([Sinergym rewards doc](https://ugr-sail.github.io/sinergym/compilation/main/pages/rewards.html));
   SB3's VecNormalize + advantage whitening is the implementation (§4.4). Studies
   that skip this get threshold-riding and instability (Sinergym SAC/TD3 traces).
4. **Action-rate penalties.** Penalize |Δu| (Zhang 2019 safety limits; SACEM;
   TreeC-style smoothing) to kill cycling/oscillation — the PPO bang-bang failure
   mode made unprofitable by construction.
5. **What NOT to do:** comfort penalty active while unoccupied (wastes energy
   holding empty buildings at setpoint — the LDLP paper's whole point); sparse
   comfort bonuses (kill gradients); unclipped violation terms (one tail event
   dominates the batch — our "no tails in hidden" finding says the leader likely
   optimized the typical case, not tails).

## 8. Recommendation: is there an RL path worth our compute?

### 8.1 Decision

**Yes, but not model-free policy learning as the primary bet.** Ranked by expected
return on our remaining compute:

**Bet 1 (primary): close the greybox/MPC loop — planning, not a policy.**
Implement receding-horizon planning (CEM or MPPI shooting) over a fast learned
dynamics model. Concretely:
- Dynamics: small ensemble MLP (PETS-style, 3–5 nets) predicting Δzone-temp +
  power from (state, action, weather, occupancy), trained on simulator rollouts
  from our occ_gated controller + random perturbations. Fallback: the greybox RC
  model if the MLP misbehaves out-of-distribution; combine as residual
  (MLP corrects greybox).
- Planner: MPPI (preferred for ≤10-zone action dims — MB²C evidence) or CEM,
  horizon 6–12 steps, replan every step; hard-project actions (damper ≥10%,
  discharge ≤308.15 K, recirc ≤0.70, |Δu| slew limit) *inside* the planner so the
  comfort cliff is structurally unreachable (§3).
- Objective: the true competition reward with occupancy gating, comfort-normalized
  energy accounting (LDLP-style) for model selection.
- Why it wins: per-state re-optimization cannot overfit a policy (§1.2 failures
  don't apply); it exploits our free simulator maximally; MPC beats all RL on
  BOPTEST and ties-or-beats on Sinergym; MPPI/MPC is the most likely shape of the
  0.00521 leader (a planner, not a policy — consistent with "no tails," since
  planners don't have tail *policies*, they just re-optimize).
- Budget: dynamics training is supervised (minutes–hours); the cost is planner
  tuning + inference-time compute. If inference time is capped, distill the
  planner into a network afterward (see Bet 3).

**Bet 2 (secondary, cheap, parallel): one disciplined SAC run — never PPO again.**
If any model-free attempt is made:
- Algorithm: **SAC with auto-tuned entropy** (prevents the bang-bang collapse we
  reproduced; PPO-collapse evidence in §4.2). TD3 as backup.
- Init: behavior-clone occ_gated7 as the starting policy / residual center
  (Gnu-RL-style); learn a *residual* Δu on top, small output scale.
- Normalization: VecNormalize obs + reward, advantage whitening, reward scale
  audit (comfort and energy terms within ~1 order of magnitude post-normalization).
- Shaping: occupancy-gated comfort (§7.2), |Δu| cycling penalty, hard action
  projection outside the policy.
- Stop rule: if it doesn't beat occ_gated7's *train* mean within a fixed step
  budget, kill it — the Sinergym 5-zone result says model-free may simply not beat
  a good RBC here, and more steps won't fix a structural mismatch.

**Bet 3 (only if Bet 1 works but is too slow): distill the planner.**
DAgger-style: run MPPI-MPC as the expert, train a small policy (SAC or plain
supervised clone) on its state→action pairs with on-policy corrections. This is
exactly Gnu-RL's "imitate then refine" order and sidesteps both the cliff (expert
never visits it) and distributional shift (expert labels the visited states).

**Explicitly not recommended:** (a) more PPO — failure mode is structural (§4.2);
(b) DreamerV3/MuZero-from-scratch — heavy machinery for pixel/long-horizon
problems we don't have, zero HVAC track record, high implementation risk for no
theoretical advantage on dense stiff rewards; (c) pure offline RL (CQL/IQL) on our
own logs — no expert data, narrow coverage, free simulator removes the point (§6);
(d) multi-agent MARLISA-style per-zone agents — MERLIN evidence says independent
SAC ≈ MARLISA and single-agent suffices, and our action dim (≤10 zones) doesn't
need factorization.

### 8.2 Why the leader is probably a planner (synthesis)

Four of our verified observations point away from "better policy" and toward
"online optimization": (i) leader beats our train mean — rules out
composition/luck; (ii) oracle/CEM finds +8–17% at simulated tails but hidden has
no tails — consistent with an opponent that re-optimizes every state rather than
memorizing tail responses; (iii) transfer suite predicted trim-respond gains that
scored 0 — policy-style tweaks don't transfer, planners do; (iv) comfort slope ≫
energy slope with TOU 1.25× dead — the knob left is *precision*: riding the comfort
boundary exactly (the TreeC/BOPTEST finding: "constantly keeping indoor temperature
just above the lower bound") without ever crossing it. That is an MPC-with-shield
behavioral signature, not a policy-gradient one. Bet 1 replicates exactly that
signature.
